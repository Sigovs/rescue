The rescue

Part of her father’s collection, she bought it from his estate and enjoyed it for more than a decade — then trusted us to find it a home that would keep it exactly as he’d left it.

The call came the way most of the good ones do — quietly, through someone who knew someone. A short-wheelbase 911S, one family since the late sixties, parked under a cover in a garage that hadn’t changed since the man who bought it new stopped driving.

These early S cars are the ones enthusiasts chase: the 160-horsepower flat-six, the Fuchs wheels, the delicacy that Porsche engineered out of the car in the decades after. Original paint, original interior, and the sort of honest patina you cannot fake and should never restore away.

“She didn’t want the highest offer. She wanted to know it would be looked after.”

We spent an afternoon with the paperwork spread across the kitchen table — the original bill of sale, service records in a man’s careful handwriting, a folder of photographs going back fifty years. That archive travels with the car now; it is as much a part of it as the engine.

It sorts, it sits in the collection, and it gets driven the way it was meant to. That was the whole point — not to flip it, but to make sure the next fifty years look after it as well as the last fifty did.
